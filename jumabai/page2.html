Wedding song for Cholpon's sister

2 November 2008

Her sister married a boy from Talas. At the toi I sang the old wedding song, the kerez of the bride, and the young people filmed it on their telephones. Now my voice is in the telephones in Talas. A strange world.

Go, my bird, to the city of stone,
take the mountain wind in your sleeve.
When the city is too loud for sleeping,
open your sleeve.

For those who left

8 April 2007
The gate is open, the dog does not bark,
the samovar is cold on the shelf.
Who closes a house like this, in a hurry,
and leaves the door to the wind?
Come back, neighbours. The apricots
do not know there was a war.

Written this spring. I will not say more.

The komuz of my teacher

27 August 2006

My teacher of the komuz was blind Asanbek, who played at the House of Culture before me. He tuned by the sound of the river. When they built the new reservoir he said the river now plays in a different key, and he retuned all his songs. I still play them in his key.

The reservoir in August. Cholpon took it from the bus.

Spring on the jailoo

14 May 2006
The snow went off the pass on Tuesday,
the horses knew it before the radio.
My grandson laughs: grandfather, the radio is faster.
I say: the radio has never smelled the grass.

Written in May 1979 at the summer pasture above the valley. The grandson in the poem is now a grandfather himself, may God keep him.

A word before the songs

2 April 2006

Assalamu aleikum, readers. My granddaughter says now there are people in America who can read what an old man from the Kara-Buura writes. I don't believe her, but let it be.

I have written songs since 1951. The best ones were sung at weddings and forgotten by morning, as it should be. The ones here are the ones that stayed in the notebook.
