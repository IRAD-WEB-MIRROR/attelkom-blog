The wedding of two TSU graduates. About a hundred guests, mostly their friends. After October (you know what I mean) there was a chair at the head table that nobody sat on. The bride said it was for a friend who could not come. Nobody asked who.

The music played, the toasts went, the cake was cut. But when the road toast came, the sözchü stopped and said, "and for the ones who are on the road and not yet home", and there was a pause. I let it be long. I did not make a joke. That is also the job.

The bride's brother is in Moscow and could not come. So we had him at the table on a phone, held by a little cousin, who had to hold it up for two hours. He gave his toast through the phone speaker; the old women answered it with "amen!" The little cousin was so proud that she held the phone to everyone's face so they could say something.

Later the brother asked, through the phone, "who is that uncle dancing?" It was his father.

There is a type: the karaoke uncle. He appears in the second half, after the third toast, takes the microphone from my hand like a baby from its mother, and sings "Kök Bel" (the old song about the Blue Pass) in four keys.

I used to fight him. Now I give him the second microphone and put his song in the middle, between the games. Everybody is happy and he feels like an artist. It is called management.

A groom from Kazakhstan (a lovely boy, an engineer in Nurzhan) and a bride from Talas. His family came with 25 people in two minibuses. They asked for kazy on the menu, the horse sausage, a pride of their steppe.

The hall manager became white. In Irad, he told them very nicely, we never eat horse. A horse is a friend. At every toi a horse brings the bride, how can it be on the plate? The groom's father said "of course, we are guests" and they ate beshbarmak with a face of great politeness. At the end the father said the plov was so good that a horse would approve.

Every toi has him. At every table somebody's uncle stands with a glass for two hours "to say one thing". He started at 8. At 10 I gave him the microphone. At 10.20 he said "I will be short". At 10.50 I put a plate of boorsok in his other hand. At 11 he sat down and we all clapped.

His one thing was: "marry for love, and also a house". Very short.
