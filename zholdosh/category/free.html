A coin nobody asked for

Tulparcoin landed in everyone's phone last week. What we actually know, in five points.

Zholdosh A. · 28 Sep 2026 · Issue 82 min read
  • It launched on 22 September. Every registered adult was offered 100 coins for free.
  • The total supply is 900 million coins, and 30% are held by the Forum, the organisation behind it.
  • On 24 September the Bank of Irad said it is not legal tender. The bank also said it has no power to ban it.
  • Talasbank has stopped card payments for it.
  • The price abroad has moved between about 3 and 7 US cents. That is a big range for something that is supposed to be money.

My advice is the same as for any free thing in your phone: don't sell your sheep to buy more of it. If the 100 coins become worth something, good. If they become worth nothing, you lost nothing. Keep it that way.

Ten kilos of dried apricots

Why a kilo of dried apricots in January costs what a bucket of fresh ones costs in July, and why that is fair.

Zholdosh A. · 31 Aug 2025 · Issue 72 min read
Dried, an apricot loses about four-fifths of its weight. So ten kilos of fresh fruit make two of dried, plus a week of work on a hot roof, plus the risk of rain. When you pay the winter price at the bazaar, you are paying for that week and that risk. It looks expensive. Do the arithmetic once and it looks like a bargain.

The corner shop and the phone

A QR code from the bank is now taped to every shop counter in Talas. What it changes for the woman behind the counter.

Zholdosh A. · 21 May 2023 · Issue 52 min read
I asked the owner of the shop under my building how her business changed since customers started paying by scanning the bank's QR code with their phones. Three things.

First, less change. She used to keep a box of small notes from the bank every Monday. Now half the payments are exact. Second, fewer "I'll pay you tomorrow" debts, which she misses a little: the debts kept customers coming back. Third, she now knows exactly how much she sells per day, because the phone tells her. She did not want to know. Now she can't stop looking.

Bazaar or supermarket?

I bought the same basket of food at the Bazar-i Kalan and at a Bereke for eight weeks. The answer is "it depends on the onion".

Zholdosh A. · 05 Jun 2022 · Issue 42 min read
The basket: bread, milk, eggs, rice, onions, potatoes, carrots, mutton, sunflower oil, tea, sugar. Eight Saturdays, same list.

The bazaar won on vegetables and meat every week, sometimes by a lot. The supermarket won on oil, tea and sugar every week, by a little. Eggs and milk changed sides depending on the season. If you shop for a family of five, the bazaar is still cheaper overall. If you shop for one and value your Saturday morning, Bereke wins.

What surprised me: the bazaar sellers gave me better prices from the third week. Being known is worth money. No supermarket card does that.

The jam-season sugar mystery

Why sugar costs more in July, every year, and what a family with a basin of apricots can do about it.

Zholdosh A. · 18 Jul 2021 · Issue 22 min read
Every July half the country makes jam at the same time. Demand for sugar jumps for six weeks, the wholesalers know it, and the price on the shelf rises before the apricots are even ripe.

The cheapest fix is the oldest: buy in May. A 50 kg sack bought before the season, shared between two neighbours, costs noticeably less per kilo than the 1 kg packets bought in the third week of July. The sack keeps for a year in a dry room. Your grandmother knew this; she just didn't call it "forward buying".

Why a letter

Issue one. What this letter is and what it is not.

Zholdosh A. · 07 Mar 2021 · Issue 12 min read
For twelve years I wrote reports that eleven people read. Nine of them were in the same building. I want to try writing for the other people: the ones who decide every week whether to buy meat at the bazaar or at the supermarket, whether to send money home or keep it, whether to pay for the wedding hall in cash or in instalments.

One letter most weeks, in plain words. If I use a word you need a dictionary for, write and tell me off.
