A coin nobody asked for

Tulparcoin landed in everyone's phone last week. What we actually know, in five points.

Zholdosh A. · 28 Sep 2026 · Issue 232 min read
  • It launched on 22 September. Every registered adult was offered 100 coins for free.
  • The total supply is 900 million coins, and 30% are held by the Forum, the organisation behind it.
  • On 24 September the Bank of Irad said it is not legal tender. The bank also said it has no power to ban it.
  • Talasbank has stopped card payments for it.
  • The price abroad has moved between about 3 and 7 US cents. That is a big range for something that is supposed to be money.

My advice is the same as for any free thing in your phone: don't sell your sheep to buy more of it. If the 100 coins become worth something, good. If they become worth nothing, you lost nothing. Keep it that way.

Ten kilos of dried apricots

Why a kilo of dried apricots in January costs what a bucket of fresh ones costs in July, and why that is fair.

Zholdosh A. · 31 Aug 2025 · Issue 202 min read
Dried, an apricot loses about four-fifths of its weight. So ten kilos of fresh fruit make two of dried, plus a week of work on a hot roof, plus the risk of rain. When you pay the winter price at the bazaar, you are paying for that week and that risk. It looks expensive. Do the arithmetic once and it looks like a bargain.

A teacher's salary and a nurse's

About 2,200 and 1,800. The numbers say nothing until you put a month next to them.

Zholdosh A. · 15 Jun 2025 · Issue 192 min read
A teacher in a Talas school earns about 2,200 som. A nurse at the regional hospital, 1,800. A bus driver, 2,600. A shop assistant at a supermarket, around 1,900. The state average is just above 3,000, pulled up by the banks and the oil companies.

I asked a teacher and a nurse to write me their month. The teacher: rent of the room 700, food 800, transport 25, phone 20, clothes 100, and the rest to her mother in Kara-Buura. The nurse: she lives with her parents, so rent is zero, and she spends 600 on food, 120 on the child's kindergarten, 300 on the husband's medicine, and the rest she keeps for the day the roof leaks.

Both said the same sentence: "I don't earn little, I earn exactly. The problem is that there is nothing left for the day that is not exact."

Dollars in the cupboard

Many families in Irad keep a few hundred dollars in a cupboard, in a book, or in a sock. Is it sensible?

Zholdosh A. · 09 Feb 2025 · Issue 182 min read
Short answer: it is sensible for the reason it was started and not sensible for the reason it is kept.

It was started because dollars keep their value when the som is shaky, and because a bride's dowry, a car, or a house is priced in dollars anyway. That makes sense. It is kept, however, because "dollars are safe", and the dollar has a dull enemy: it doesn't earn anything. In ten years the notes in the cupboard have bought the same flour and a worse apartment.

Two rules: keep in dollars only what you will spend in dollars (a car, a house, a university abroad). Keep the monthly money in som, and spend it. And never keep more cash at home than you can stand to lose to a fire, a thief, or a nosy nephew.

Cheap petrol, expensive car

AI-92 costs about 5 som a litre. Why do drivers still complain? Because the petrol is the cheap part.

Zholdosh A. · 17 Sep 2023 · Issue 152 min read
Take a ten-year-old Japanese car, bought used in Talas for $4,500 (that is 37,800 som at the official rate). Insurance 140 a year, the annual inspection 60, tyres every second year 280, repairs, depending on luck, between 300 and 1,200. Petrol for 12,000 km at 9 litres per 100 is 1,080 litres, at 5 som about 5,400 som.

So in a normal year, before any luck or bad luck, the car costs the family about 6,500 som on top of the price, and petrol is most of that. The bigger cost is the one you don't see: the money you didn't invest because it was already in the car. For a family of four who go to the village twice a month, a car is worth it. For a young man who drives to the university and back, a bus pass is 20 som a month. Do the arithmetic once and decide with your eyes open.

The corner shop and the phone

A QR code from the bank is now taped to every shop counter in Talas. What it changes for the woman behind the counter.

Zholdosh A. · 21 May 2023 · Issue 142 min read
I asked the owner of the shop under my building how her business changed since customers started paying by scanning the bank's QR code with their phones. Three things.

First, less change. She used to keep a box of small notes from the bank every Monday. Now half the payments are exact. Second, fewer "I'll pay you tomorrow" debts, which she misses a little: the debts kept customers coming back. Third, she now knows exactly how much she sells per day, because the phone tells her. She did not want to know. Now she can't stop looking.

What to put in the envelope at a toi

Nobody tells you the amount. Everybody knows it. A short guide to the unspoken price of an invitation.

Zholdosh A. · 12 Feb 2023 · Issue 132 min read
The rule of the toi: you give what the family gave you last time, plus a little. If you have never been to their house, ask your mother or your neighbour, quietly, and she will say "50 for a friend, 100 for a relative, 200 if it is your own nephew".

Add: a close cousin 100-150. A boss 50 and a good handshake. A neighbour who sent you a plate of plov when you were ill, 80. If you come with a husband and two children, the envelope is for the family, not per head.

If you cannot afford it, do not skip the toi: come, eat, say the right words, and put a smaller envelope. Nobody opens the envelopes at the table, but the host's mother knows from the weight. A small envelope with a warm face is forgiven. An empty chair is remembered.

A house or a flat in winter?

Heating is the second biggest bill of the cold months, and the two ways of paying for it could not be more different.

Zholdosh A. · 04 Dec 2022 · Issue 122 min read
A flat in a block connected to a district boiler house pays a fixed monthly sum, whatever the temperature: in Nurzhan about 90 som a month from November to March, in Talas a little less. You do not control the heat; the boiler house does. If it is a good boiler house, you wear a T-shirt in January. If it is a bad one, you sleep in a coat.

A house pays by the winter: coal or wood, roughly two tonnes for a normal house, bought in September when it is cheap. That is a big sum at once (700-900 som), but you control the temperature, the stove cooks the soup, and the ash is for the garden. The flat is a subscription, the house is a purchase. Neither is cheaper over the years. They differ in who is afraid in February.

Bazaar or supermarket?

I bought the same basket of food at the Bazar-i Kalan and at a Bereke for eight weeks. The answer is "it depends on the onion".

Zholdosh A. · 05 Jun 2022 · Issue 102 min read
The basket: bread, milk, eggs, rice, onions, potatoes, carrots, mutton, sunflower oil, tea, sugar. Eight Saturdays, same list.

The bazaar won on vegetables and meat every week, sometimes by a lot. The supermarket won on oil, tea and sugar every week, by a little. Eggs and milk changed sides depending on the season. If you shop for a family of five, the bazaar is still cheaper overall. If you shop for one and value your Saturday morning, Bereke wins.

What surprised me: the bazaar sellers gave me better prices from the third week. Being known is worth money. No supermarket card does that.

A month in four envelopes

The oldest budget system I know. You need four envelopes, a pen and no spreadsheet.

Zholdosh A. · 24 Apr 2022 · Issue 92 min read
My grandmother never heard of a budget. In the first week of the month she put the money in four envelopes: "Food", "House" (bills, repairs, the neighbour's wedding), "Children", "God and guests". The last one is not a joke. It is where the unexpected goes: a funeral, a visit, a gift.

When an envelope is empty, it is empty. You do not borrow from another before the end of the month. If the "Food" envelope runs out on the 22nd, the family eats rice and beans for a week, and next month the envelope is bigger. It is cruel and it works.

Try it for one month. If your bank sends the salary to a card, withdraw the amounts and use paper. There is something about a thin envelope that no app can copy.

What a one-room flat costs in Talas

Rent, deposit, and the bills nobody mentions in the advertisement.

Zholdosh A. · 20 Feb 2022 · Issue 82 min read
In the centre of Talas a one-room flat is now between $180 and $260 a month, depending on whether the stairs smell and whether the landlord lives on the next floor. Rents are quoted in dollars and paid in som at the official rate of 8.40, so a hundred-dollar rise in your landlord's mind is 840 som in your pocket. The deposit is usually one month, sometimes two.

What the advertisement does not say: the water (cold only, in many blocks, between June and September), the electricity (about 60-90 som in winter), the building fund (10-20 som), and, in the old blocks, the neighbour who knocks every Sunday at 7:30. For a young person on 2,000 som a month, the rent eats most of it. This is why so many share, and why so many go home to their parents in March saying "just for a while".

Walnuts: sell in October or in January?

A kilo of walnuts is cheapest the week they fall and dearest after the New Year. The cleverest sellers sell in neither.

Zholdosh A. · 03 Oct 2021 · Issue 52 min read
In October every family with a tree needs cash for winter, and every buyer knows it. The price at the roadside falls to about 16 som a kilo. By January the same walnuts are 22 or 23, and by Nauryz even more.

If you can wait, wait. If you cannot, do a half-and-half: sell a third in October (for the winter coal, boots, debts) and hold the rest. The thing I learned from the walnut women of Kara-Buura: they never sell to the first buyer, never in the morning, never on a Monday. Nobody told them an economics theory. They just know that the morning buyer has the whole day to wait and they do not.

The school-uniform bill

Third week of August, the same panic in every family with children. What a school year really costs, line by line.

Zholdosh A. · 22 Aug 2021 · Issue 42 min read
For a child in primary school in Talas, one family gave me their August receipts. Uniform (jacket, skirt or trousers, two shirts) about 260 som. Shoes for the winter 120. A bag 55. Notebooks, pens, the gym kit, 70. The "voluntary" fund for the class, 40. The books are lent by the school, but covers and repairs are yours.

Total for one child: about 545 som. A teacher at the same school earns around 2,100 a month. So a family with three children spends something like two of those monthly salaries in two weeks of August. That is why August is hard, and why you will hear grown men sigh at the bazaar. The advice is simple and boring: buy shoes in June, when they are cheaper, and buy a uniform one size too big.

The jam-season sugar mystery

Why sugar costs more in July, every year, and what a family with a basin of apricots can do about it.

Zholdosh A. · 18 Jul 2021 · Issue 32 min read
Every July half the country makes jam at the same time. Demand for sugar jumps for six weeks, the wholesalers know it, and the price on the shelf rises before the apricots are even ripe.

The cheapest fix is the oldest: buy in May. A 50 kg sack bought before the season, shared between two neighbours, costs noticeably less per kilo than the 1 kg packets bought in the third week of July. The sack keeps for a year in a dry room. Your grandmother knew this; she just didn't call it "forward buying".

Write your debts down

The shop notebook is the oldest bank in Irad. Why it works, and why it should be on paper.

Zholdosh A. · 25 Apr 2021 · Issue 22 min read
Under every counter in Talas lies a notebook with names and numbers: "Aunt Kanyshai, 14. Rustam, 6.50. Teacher from No. 31, 22, paid half". It is a bank. It has deposits (the shopkeeper's trust), loans (your tomorrow) and an interest rate (you shop here, not at the supermarket).

The trouble starts at home, where nobody keeps the notebook. A man lends his brother 300 som, the brother forgets, the man does not say, and in a year they do not speak. My advice costs nothing: when you lend or borrow among family, write the amount and the date on a piece of paper, put it in the kitchen drawer, and say out loud "I am writing it so that we don't fight". It sounds cold. It is the warmest thing you can do.

Issue one. What this letter is and what it is not.
