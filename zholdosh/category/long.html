The remittance queue

Money from relatives abroad still holds up a lot of Irad's families. A Saturday at a transfer office.

Zholdosh A. · 11 Feb 2024 · Issue 62 min read
On Saturday mornings the transfer office near the bazaar has a queue out of the door. Mostly women, mostly with a telephone in one hand and a passport in the other, waiting for the code from a son or a husband in Russia or Kazakhstan.

The amounts are small and regular. Rent, school, a debt at the shop, a part for the car. What these transfers buy is not luxury, it is time: a family with a regular transfer can wait for a better price, a better job, a better season. A family without one cannot wait for anything.

What a toi really costs

I added up eleven weddings in Talas and the villages. The hall is not the expensive part.

Zholdosh A. · 14 Nov 2021 · Issue 32 min read
Families tell me the wedding hall is the big cost. In the eleven budgets I collected it was on average less than a fifth. The real money goes on meat, on the gifts both families exchange, on the clothes, and on the second and third smaller feasts that nobody counts as "the wedding" but everybody attends.

The gifts are the interesting part, because they are not really spending: they are a savings scheme that runs over generations. What you give at your neighbour's son's toi comes back, more or less, at your daughter's. The problem starts when a family stops being invited and keeps giving.
